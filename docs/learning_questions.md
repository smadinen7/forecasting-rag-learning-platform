# Learning Questions

This document outlines the seven core learning questions that guide the Personal Learning Portal for Corporate Forecasting & Trend Breaks.

## 1. What are structural breaks and why do they matter in financial forecasting?

Understanding the fundamental concept of structural breaks (regime changes, trend breaks) in time series data and their critical impact on forecast accuracy, model stability, and risk management in corporate finance.

## 2. How can we detect structural breaks in real-time vs. retrospectively?

Exploring the methods, algorithms, and statistical tests for identifying breaks, including the trade-offs between online (real-time) detection and offline (retrospective) analysis, and their practical implications for forecasting pipelines.

## 3. What overlay mechanisms improve forecasting under regime uncertainty?

Investigating techniques such as regime-switching models, ensemble methods, Bayesian model averaging, and adaptive filtering that allow forecasts to remain robust when the underlying data-generating process shifts.

## 4. How do nowcasting techniques adapt to sudden macroeconomic shifts?

Examining high-frequency data integration, mixed-frequency models (MIDAS), and dynamic factor models that enable timely forecasts during periods of rapid change (e.g., pandemics, policy shocks, market crashes).

## 5. What evaluation metrics are appropriate for forecasting under breaks?

Learning specialized metrics beyond standard MSE/MAE, including break-adjusted error measures, regime-conditional performance, and robustness scores that account for structural instability.

## 6. How should governance and monitoring evolve in break-aware systems?

Understanding the operational, ethical, and compliance considerations for deploying automated break detection and adaptive forecasting in production, including model versioning, audit trails, and stakeholder communication.

## 7. What reflective practices enhance learning about forecast failures?

Developing meta-cognitive skills to analyze why forecasts fail during breaks, documenting lessons learned, and iteratively improving detection and overlay strategies through structured reflection and experimentation.
